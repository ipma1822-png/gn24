New Zealand Government Moves to Disestablish Broadcasting Standards Authority

Legislation introduced to Parliament signals a major restructuring of the country’s broadcasting regulation framework
2026-09-26 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

WELLINGTON — The New Zealand Government has introduced legislation to Parliament that would disestablish the Broadcasting Standards Authority (BSA), setting the stage for a significant change in the country’s media regulatory landscape.

Media and Communications Minister Paul Goldsmith announced the introduction of the legislation on 22 September 2026.

The BSA has long played a role in overseeing broadcasting standards and considering complaints relating to television and radio content. The proposed restructuring comes as traditional broadcasting increasingly operates alongside streaming platforms, social media and other forms of digital content.

Changes in technology have transformed the way New Zealanders consume news and entertainment. Television and radio broadcasters now compete for audiences with global streaming services, online video platforms and social networks, creating new questions about how regulatory systems designed for conventional broadcasting should operate in the digital era.

The introduction of the legislation begins the parliamentary process rather than completing the change. The proposal will therefore be subject to New Zealand’s legislative procedures and political scrutiny.

The debate is likely to focus on how broadcasting standards and public accountability should be maintained while regulation adapts to a media environment that increasingly crosses the boundaries between broadcasting, online publishing and global digital platforms.

For New Zealand’s media sector, the legislation represents another stage in a broader transition from traditional broadcasting structures toward a more digitally integrated communications environment.
