Canada Announces $52.5 Billion AI Infrastructure Investment in Saskatchewan

Bell Canada's planned expansion could create a 1.2-gigawatt AI hub and 4,500 jobs, strengthening Canada's sovereign computing capacity.
2026-09-25 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

CANADA | GLOBAL NEWS24

Canada is taking a significant step toward expanding its domestic artificial intelligence infrastructure following the announcement of Bell Canada's planned investment of up to $52.5 billion in Saskatchewan.

On September 14, 2026, the Canadian government welcomed the project, which is expected to expand Bell AI Fabric and establish a major Canadian AI infrastructure hub.

A MAJOR INVESTMENT IN ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

According to the Canadian government, Bell's planned expansion could provide up to 900 megawatts of additional computing infrastructure, creating a pathway toward a 1.2-gigawatt AI hub.

The proposed investment of up to $52.5 billion would represent the largest capital investment in Saskatchewan's history.

The project is also expected to generate approximately 4,500 jobs across construction, operations and related activities.

STRENGTHENING CANADA'S DIGITAL INFRASTRUCTURE

The investment forms part of Canada's broader efforts to develop sovereign computing capacity and strengthen its position in the global artificial intelligence industry.

Large-scale data centres provide the computing and storage resources required for advanced AI systems, scientific research, financial services, health care and digital government services.

Expanding domestic computing infrastructure could also help Canadian businesses and research institutions gain greater access to advanced AI capabilities.

RESPONSIBLE DATA CENTRE DEVELOPMENT

The Canadian government has emphasized the importance of developing AI infrastructure responsibly.

Its Responsible Data Centre Development Principles address electricity affordability, environmental impacts, water consumption, transparency and long-term benefits for local communities.

As AI computing requirements continue to increase, access to reliable electricity and sustainable infrastructure will remain important considerations for large-scale data centre projects.

A NEW CHAPTER FOR CANADA'S AI ECONOMY

The Saskatchewan project comes as Canada seeks to attract international investment in advanced technology, energy and digital infrastructure.

The government has also been working with provincial authorities, electricity providers and technology companies to explore opportunities for additional computing capacity across the country.

While the full investment and employment benefits will depend on the project's implementation, the announcement highlights Canada's growing ambitions in the global artificial intelligence industry.
