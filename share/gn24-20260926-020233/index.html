Smithsonian Marks America’s 250th Anniversary with Nationwide History and Culture Initiative

“Our Shared Future: 250” uses exhibitions and storytelling to examine the country’s past, present and evolving national identity
2026-09-26 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

WASHINGTON — The United States’ 250th anniversary is continuing to shape museum exhibitions, educational programs and public discussions about the country’s history throughout 2026.

The Smithsonian Institution launched a major national initiative titled “Our Shared Future: 250” to commemorate the semiquincentennial — 250 years since the adoption of the Declaration of Independence on July 4, 1776.

The program brings together exhibitions, events and storytelling projects designed to examine the people, ideas and experiences that have shaped the United States.

The anniversary is more than a celebration of a historical date. It has also created an opportunity for museums and cultural institutions to revisit how American history is presented to contemporary audiences.

The Smithsonian describes its role as documenting the country’s achievements, scientific innovation and social development while exploring the nation’s continuing efforts to realize the ideals expressed at its founding.

That approach gives the anniversary a broad cultural dimension.

American history encompasses political institutions and wars, but also immigration, Indigenous history, civil rights, science, technology, music, art, sports and the experiences of communities whose stories have not always occupied equal space in traditional historical narratives.

Museums can therefore serve as important meeting places between historical scholarship and public memory.

The Smithsonian occupies a particularly prominent position in that process. Its museums, research centers and collections cover subjects ranging from American history and African American history to aerospace, natural science and art.

The 250th anniversary also arrives at a moment when the United States is experiencing significant debates over national identity, citizenship, historical interpretation and the country’s future direction.

Rather than resolving those debates, museums and educational institutions can provide documents, objects and historical context that allow visitors to examine how the nation developed over two and a half centuries.

For international visitors, the semiquincentennial year also offers a distinctive window into American society — not only through monuments and national ceremonies, but through the diverse histories represented in museums and communities across the country.
