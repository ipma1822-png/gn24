Kenya’s Inflation Rises to 6.59% as Cost Pressures Remain in Focus

August consumer-price data highlights renewed attention on household costs and economic conditions
2026-09-26 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

NAIROBI — Kenya’s annual inflation rate rose to 6.59% in August 2026, keeping the cost of living among the key economic issues facing households and policymakers.

Data published by the Central Bank of Kenya, sourced from the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics, shows that 12-month inflation increased from 6.49% in July to 6.59% in August.

The figures indicate a notable change from earlier in the year. Annual inflation stood at 4.40% in January and 4.39% in March before accelerating in subsequent months.

The annual average inflation rate also continued to rise, reaching 5.25% in August.

Inflation is closely watched in Kenya because changes in food, transport, housing and energy costs can have an immediate effect on household budgets, particularly for lower-income families.

Price developments also influence monetary-policy decisions, business planning and consumer confidence.

Kenya’s broader economic challenge is to maintain growth while managing living costs, public finances and investment requirements.

The government’s 2026/27 budget authorises expenditure of KSh4.82 trillion, placing significant attention on how public resources are allocated while the country continues to address infrastructure and development priorities.

For businesses, the direction of inflation over the coming months will remain important for operating costs, wage pressures and consumer demand.

For households, however, the economic discussion is more immediate: the purchasing power of income and the cost of everyday essentials.
