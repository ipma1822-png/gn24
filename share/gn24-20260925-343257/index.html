Canada and Vietnam Open Direct Air Links, Expanding Indo-Pacific Connectivity

Expanded air transport agreement strengthens travel, trade and business links between Canada and its largest ASEAN trading partner.
2026-09-25 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

CANADA | GLOBAL NEWS24

Canada is expanding its aviation connections with Southeast Asia as direct air links between Canada and Vietnam begin under an expanded bilateral air transport agreement.

On September 24, 2026, the Government of Canada highlighted the development as part of its broader effort to diversify international trade, improve global connectivity and strengthen economic relations with the Indo-Pacific region.

CONNECTING CANADA AND VIETNAM

The expanded air transport relationship is expected to create new opportunities for passengers, businesses and shippers travelling between the two countries.

Direct connectivity can reduce travel complexity and support growing demand for tourism, business, education and family visits.

Vietnam is Canada's largest trading partner within the Association of Southeast Asian Nations, making improved transportation links strategically important for Canada's growing engagement with Southeast Asia.

STRONG PEOPLE-TO-PEOPLE TIES

Canada and Vietnam also share significant social and cultural connections.

According to the Canadian government, approximately 275,000 people of Vietnamese origin live in Canada.

These established communities contribute to demand for travel between the two countries for family visits, tourism, education and business.

Improved air connectivity could therefore strengthen not only commercial relations but also the long-standing links between Canadian and Vietnamese communities.

A GATEWAY TO THE INDO-PACIFIC

The Indo-Pacific has become an increasingly important region for Canada's international trade and investment strategy.

Canada is seeking to diversify its economic relationships and develop new markets beyond its traditional trading partners.

Vietnam's rapidly developing economy and its position within Southeast Asia make the country an important partner in that strategy.

Expanded aviation links can support this relationship by making the movement of people and goods faster and more convenient.

OPPORTUNITIES FOR TRADE AND TOURISM

International air connectivity plays an important role in modern economies.

Direct routes can help businesses reach overseas markets, support international tourism and improve access for high-value and time-sensitive cargo.

For Canadian companies seeking opportunities in Southeast Asia, stronger transportation links with Vietnam may provide additional access to one of the region's growing markets.

Vietnamese businesses and travellers could similarly benefit from improved access to Canadian destinations and markets.

CANADA EXPANDS ITS GLOBAL AVIATION NETWORK

Canada continues to expand its network of international air transport agreements.

According to Transport Canada, the country currently has air transport agreements or arrangements covering more than 125 countries.

These agreements establish the framework under which airlines can develop international services and expand passenger and cargo connections.

The Canada-Vietnam development represents another step in the expansion of this global aviation network.

BEYOND AVIATION

The new connectivity comes as Canada and Vietnam are also working to deepen their broader bilateral relationship.

The two countries are exploring increased cooperation in areas including trade, investment, technology, transportation, energy and agri-food.

Improved air connections can provide practical infrastructure supporting these wider economic and diplomatic relationships.

CONNECTING CANADA WITH A CHANGING ASIA

As global trade patterns continue to evolve, transportation links are becoming increasingly important to national economic strategies.

For Canada, stronger connections with Vietnam could provide another bridge between North America and Southeast Asia while supporting tourism, business and international exchange.

The long-term impact will depend on airline operations, passenger demand and the continued development of bilateral economic relations.
