New Zealand Economy Shows Modest Growth as Central Bank Raises Interest Rate

June-quarter GDP expands by 0.2% while inflation pressures keep monetary policy in focus
2026-09-26 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

AUCKLAND — New Zealand’s economy is showing signs of a gradual recovery, although growth remains uneven and inflation continues to influence monetary policy.

Official data released in September showed that gross domestic product expanded by 0.2% in the June 2026 quarter. The result pointed to continued economic activity, but at a relatively subdued pace.

The Reserve Bank of New Zealand has meanwhile increased the Official Cash Rate by 25 basis points to 2.75%.

According to the central bank’s September Monetary Policy Statement, annual consumer price inflation reached 4.1% in the June quarter, with higher fuel prices associated with the conflict in the Middle East contributing to the increase.

The Reserve Bank said underlying indicators including core inflation, expected wage growth and inflation expectations remained consistent with inflation eventually returning to its 1–3% target range.

Economic conditions are not uniform across the country. Strong export prices and demand from major trading partners have supported incomes and investment in export-oriented industries and regional areas.

Household spending and residential investment, however, have remained under pressure from weak income growth, employment uncertainty and relatively flat house prices, particularly in Auckland and Wellington.

The central bank expects the recovery to continue, while the combination of inflation, interest rates, global energy prices and domestic demand will remain important factors shaping New Zealand’s economic outlook through the remainder of 2026 and into 2027.
