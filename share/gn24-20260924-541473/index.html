ndia and Korea: Culture, Education and Sports Build New Bridges Between People

Youth exchange, education, culture, sports and martial arts can create new opportunities for people in India and Korea to understand one another and build lasting international connections.
2026-09-24 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

GLOBAL NEWS24 · INDIA EDITION

India and the Republic of Korea are separated by thousands of kilometers, but modern communication, education, culture and sports are creating new opportunities for people in the two countries to connect.

International exchange is no longer limited to governments, large institutions or major business organizations.

Students, teachers, athletes, coaches, artists and young people can now communicate directly through digital platforms, educational programs, cultural activities and international sporting events.

Culture is one of the most natural ways to understand another country.

India has a remarkably diverse cultural landscape shaped by its long history, languages, regional traditions, music, dance, food, art and festivals.

Korea also has a distinctive cultural heritage alongside a modern cultural presence that has reached audiences around the world.

When people experience another country's music, food, art or traditions, they gain an opportunity to look beyond geographical distance and discover how other communities live and think.

Education can deepen that connection.

Language learning, university programs, academic exchanges and online education can help students from India and Korea share knowledge and experiences.

Digital technology has made this process increasingly accessible.

A student does not always need to travel internationally to begin an international exchange.

Online classes, video conferences and collaborative projects can connect classrooms and young people across borders.

Sports offer another universal language.

Athletes may speak different languages, but training, competition, teamwork and respect are values that can be understood across cultures.

India has a diverse sporting environment, with cricket holding a particularly prominent place alongside many other sports.

Korea also has a strong sporting culture and is internationally recognized as the birthplace of taekwondo.

Martial arts can therefore provide another meaningful field of exchange.

India has its own martial traditions, while taekwondo and other international martial arts are practiced by participants in many countries.

Seminars, training programs, demonstrations, competitions and online activities can provide opportunities for athletes and instructors to meet and learn from one another.

For young participants, however, the value of international sports should not be measured only by medals.

Learning discipline, respecting opponents, developing confidence and forming friendships with participants from other cultures can be equally important.

Technology is making these exchanges possible in new ways.

Digital competitions, online training and international networks can allow young people to participate in global activities even when travel is difficult.

This can open international experiences to a wider group of students and athletes.

The younger generation will play an important role in the future relationship between India and Korea.

Young people who learn about different cultures and communicate with international friends may develop a broader understanding of the world around them.

Media can also contribute by telling these stories.

International news does not have to focus only on political or economic events.

Stories involving students, teachers, athletes, coaches and communities can also help people understand another country.

GLOBAL NEWS24 INDIA EDITION will continue to introduce cultural, educational, sporting and youth activities that connect India, Korea and the wider world.

The strongest international connections often begin not with institutions, but with people meeting, learning and growing together.
