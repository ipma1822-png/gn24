Australia Watches Inflation Closely as Reserve Bank Keeps Cash Rate at 4.35%

July consumer prices rose 3.5% over the year, keeping cost-of-living pressures and monetary policy firmly in focus
2026-09-26 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

SYDNEY — Inflation and interest rates remain central concerns for Australia’s economy as households and businesses continue to navigate elevated living and operating costs.

The Australian Bureau of Statistics reported that the Consumer Price Index increased 3.5% over the 12 months to July 2026, easing from 3.8% in the year to June.

Housing recorded an annual increase of 5.0%, while food and non-alcoholic beverages rose 3.2% and recreation and culture increased 2.6%, according to the ABS.

Underlying inflation also remained elevated, with the trimmed mean measure at 3.6% over the year to July.

Against that backdrop, the Reserve Bank of Australia left its cash rate target unchanged at 4.35% at its August monetary policy meeting.

Interest rates have wide-ranging consequences across the Australian economy. Mortgage repayments affect household budgets, while borrowing costs influence business investment, housing construction and consumer spending.

The next rounds of inflation and monetary policy data will therefore be watched closely by financial markets, businesses and households.

Australia’s economic challenge is increasingly centred on bringing inflation under control while maintaining employment and avoiding an unnecessarily sharp slowdown in economic activity.

How quickly inflation moderates will be a key factor shaping the country’s economic outlook through the remainder of 2026.
