Canada Moves to Expand Role in Global Carbon Markets and Carbon Removal Industry

Federal government explores a new framework for international carbon trading as Canada seeks investment in carbon removal technologies and nature-based climate solutions.
2026-09-25 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

CANADA | GLOBAL NEWS24

Canada is taking steps toward a larger role in international carbon markets as the country seeks to attract investment in carbon-removal technologies and other climate-related industries.

On September 24, 2026, Environment and Climate Change Canada announced that the federal government is exploring a policy framework for trading internationally transferred mitigation outcomes, commonly known as ITMOs.

The proposed approach could provide Canadian companies with new opportunities to participate in international carbon markets while supporting investment in projects designed to reduce or remove greenhouse gas emissions.

WHAT ARE ITMOs?

Internationally transferred mitigation outcomes are emissions reductions or removals that can be transferred between countries under international climate accounting frameworks.

They form part of the mechanisms established under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement, which provides avenues for countries to cooperate in meeting climate targets.

Canada's proposed framework would examine how Canadian businesses and projects could participate in these international markets while maintaining appropriate accounting and environmental standards.

A NEW MARKET FOR CANADIAN TECHNOLOGY

The federal government says Canada has several advantages that could support the development of a globally competitive carbon-removal industry.

These include industrial expertise, geological resources, clean electricity, natural resources and an established climate-policy framework.

Carbon removal can involve a range of approaches.

Technology-based methods may capture carbon dioxide directly from industrial facilities or the atmosphere, while nature-based approaches can use forests, soils and other ecosystems to absorb and store carbon.

ATTRACTING INTERNATIONAL INVESTMENT

International carbon markets could potentially create additional sources of financing for Canadian emissions-reduction and carbon-removal projects.

Under such systems, verified emissions reductions or removals may have economic value when transferred internationally.

For Canadian companies developing carbon-management technologies, access to international markets could expand potential customers and investment opportunities.

The federal government says international cooperation could help accelerate deployment of carbon-removal technologies and nature-based climate solutions.

CANADA'S INDUSTRIAL OPPORTUNITY

The development of carbon-removal industries is increasingly connected to broader questions of industrial competitiveness.

Companies operating in international markets are facing growing pressure to reduce emissions across manufacturing, transportation, energy and supply chains.

Canada is seeking to position domestic technology and natural-resource industries to compete as this transition develops.

Carbon capture, utilization and storage, direct-air capture and nature-based carbon removal could become increasingly important components of this emerging market.

FROM CLIMATE POLICY TO ECONOMIC STRATEGY

Canada's latest announcement also illustrates how climate policy is becoming increasingly connected with investment and international trade.

Rather than viewing emissions reduction only as an environmental issue, governments and businesses are examining how clean technologies and carbon-management services could become part of future industrial markets.

Canada's potential participation in international carbon trading would therefore have implications beyond environmental policy.

It could influence technology development, investment, natural-resource projects and Canada's economic relationships with international partners.

FRAMEWORK STILL UNDER DEVELOPMENT

The federal announcement represents the development of a policy framework rather than the launch of a completed international trading system.

Details concerning eligibility, accounting rules, environmental integrity and participation by Canadian companies will be important as the framework develops.

The effectiveness of international carbon markets also depends on credible measurement and verification of emissions reductions and removals.

CANADA LOOKS TOWARD A LOW-CARBON GLOBAL ECONOMY

As countries and industries pursue lower-emissions economic models, competition is growing around clean technology, renewable energy and carbon-management solutions.

Canada is seeking to use its energy resources, natural environment, scientific expertise and industrial capacity to participate in these emerging markets.

The proposed international carbon-market framework could become another component of that strategy.
