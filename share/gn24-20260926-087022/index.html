New Zealand Tourism Recovery Strengthens as International Visitor Market Rebuilds

Latest visitor data point to renewed international demand while the sector looks beyond pandemic-era disruption
2026-09-26 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

AORAKI / MOUNT COOK — New Zealand’s tourism industry is continuing to rebuild its international visitor base as travellers return to the country’s cities, national parks and regional destinations.

The Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment released updated International Visitor Survey results in September covering the year ending June 2026.

The data provide annual information for major markets including Australia, China, the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany and Canada, as well as broader regional visitor groups.

Tourism remains an important component of New Zealand’s international economy, supporting accommodation, aviation, hospitality, transport and a wide range of businesses in regional communities.

Australia remains particularly important because of its geographic proximity and extensive air connections with New Zealand, while the recovery of long-haul Asian and North American travel adds further diversity to the visitor market.

The renewed growth also brings management challenges at some of the country’s most popular natural attractions.

In September, the Department of Conservation announced that a special “pause gate” would be installed on the first bridge of the Hooker Valley Track at Aoraki/Mount Cook to help manage visitor numbers.

The measure illustrates the balance New Zealand faces as tourism recovers: attracting international visitors while protecting landscapes and maintaining safe, sustainable access to heavily visited natural areas.

The long-term direction of the industry will therefore depend not only on visitor numbers but also on spending, regional distribution, infrastructure and the environmental management of destinations that form the foundation of New Zealand’s global tourism image.
