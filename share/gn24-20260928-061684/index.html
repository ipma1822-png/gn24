Canada Accelerates Sovereign AI Infrastructure Push with Major Saskatchewan Expansion

Planned computing hub could reach 1.2 gigawatts as Canada seeks greater domestic control over critical AI capacity
2026-09-28 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

Canada is accelerating investment in large-scale artificial intelligence infrastructure as demand for computing capacity becomes an increasingly important part of economic competitiveness and national digital strategy.

The federal government recently welcomed Bell Canada’s planned expansion of its AI infrastructure in Saskatchewan, describing the project as an important step toward increasing sovereign computing capacity.

According to Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada, the planned expansion could provide up to 900 megawatts of additional capacity and create a path toward a 1.2-gigawatt Canadian AI infrastructure hub.

The project could involve capital investment of up to C$52.5 billion and support thousands of jobs if fully developed.

Large data centres increasingly support artificial intelligence, health care, financial services, scientific research, education and government systems. Canadian policymakers have emphasized that domestic computing infrastructure can also help ensure that critical data and technology operate under Canadian laws and governance.

Canada has several advantages in attracting AI infrastructure investment, including significant energy resources, available land, a relatively cool climate and an established technology and research ecosystem.

The expansion also highlights a broader challenge facing countries around the world: AI development increasingly depends not only on software and algorithms but also on electricity, data centres, semiconductor access and large-scale computing infrastructure.

As global competition for AI capacity intensifies, Canada is positioning domestic computing infrastructure as both an economic opportunity and a strategic national asset.
