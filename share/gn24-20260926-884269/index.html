South Africa Records Rhino Population Growth, but Poaching Remains a Major Threat

National rhino population rose to at least 15,096 by the end of 2025 as authorities intensify conservation and anti-poaching measures
2026-09-26 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

KRUGER NATIONAL PARK — South Africa has recorded growth in its rhino population, offering an encouraging sign for conservation efforts while highlighting the continuing threat posed by poaching.

According to the Department of Forestry, Fisheries and the Environment, the country’s minimum national rhino population was estimated at 15,096 animals at the end of 2025.

The total included at least 2,565 black rhinos and 12,531 white rhinos and represented a 4.9% increase from 2024.

South Africa remains home to the world’s largest rhino population, making developments in the country central to global efforts to conserve the species.

Despite the population increase, poaching remains a serious challenge.

From January through the end of August 2026, 230 rhinos were poached across the country, compared with 248 during the same period in 2025.

Kruger National Park alone lost 114 rhinos during the first eight months of the year, down from 123 during the corresponding period a year earlier.

Authorities have combined multiple approaches to protect remaining populations, including strengthened ranger capacity, monitoring, dehorning programmes, technology and law-enforcement measures.

South Africa also published a 10-year Biodiversity Management Plan for black and white rhinos in April 2026.

The latest figures demonstrate the complex state of rhino conservation: populations can recover when protection and breeding efforts succeed, but organised wildlife crime continues to threaten those gains.

Maintaining the recovery will require long-term conservation funding, effective law enforcement and cooperation between national parks, private reserves, local communities and international partners.
