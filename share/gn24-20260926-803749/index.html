Kenya Pushes Digital Infrastructure, Data Centres and AI in Bid for Bigger African Technology Role

Government places fibre networks, cloud computing and artificial intelligence at the centre of digital investment strategy
2026-09-26 · Global News24 편집부 · Global News24

NAIROBI — Kenya is stepping up efforts to position itself as a major African digital infrastructure hub, with fibre connectivity, data centres, cloud computing and artificial intelligence becoming increasingly important parts of the country’s development strategy.

The government outlined the priorities during the ITW Africa and DataCloud Africa Summit held in Nairobi in September.

Officials said digital infrastructure should increasingly be regarded as critical national infrastructure, reflecting the growing dependence of businesses and public services on reliable connectivity and computing capacity.

Kenya already has one of East Africa’s most developed technology ecosystems, supported by mobile financial services, a growing startup sector, international connectivity and Nairobi’s role as a regional corporate centre.

The next phase is increasingly focused on the infrastructure behind those services.

Data centres provide the computing capacity required for cloud services, digital payments, government platforms, artificial intelligence applications and the storage and processing of rapidly expanding volumes of data.

Expansion of fibre networks can also improve connectivity beyond major urban centres, potentially widening access to online education, financial services, e-commerce and digital public services.

Artificial intelligence is emerging as another part of the discussion. Kenya is examining how AI can support economic activity and service delivery while governments around the world simultaneously debate issues including data protection, skills, cybersecurity and responsible AI governance.

The development of Kenya’s digital economy will therefore depend not only on technology investment but also on reliable electricity, skilled workers, regulatory certainty and cybersecurity.

As competition for digital investment increases across Africa, Kenya is seeking to translate its established technology reputation into a broader infrastructure advantage.
